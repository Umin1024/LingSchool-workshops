2025年初，我在中国的网络社群“灵感买家俱乐部”）办了一场关于数据化的工作坊。Undatable——un-data-ble 它指向无法被数据化的经验，但不得不被数据化的当下。想在大家讨论大数据生活之前，聚焦回数据化这一过程本身。它探索的是符号和符号背后内容之间的差距，具体而又细致的过程本身。它尝试探索那些不可被数据化的经验，记忆，在数字化的过程当中的失真的具体过程。

7 位来自不同背景的朋友——音乐播客主播、艺术家、即兴表演者、公司咨询师等——加入了这场实验。我们用研讨、游戏和动手实验，各自用不同的方法，定义并演示自己的"数据化"。

它起始于我的几个问题:
机器观看和人眼观看，究竟有什么不同？
如果记录我的不是机器传感器，而是亲密的家人和朋友——他们眼中的我、被他们记录下来的我，又会是怎样的?
数据化自己，到底意味着什么?
而对于"数据化"这件事本身，我们还能有怎样的想象?

In early 2025, I hosted a workshop on datafication within the Chinese online community "Ling School"） — Undatable — un-data-ble — points to experiences that cannot be datafied, yet must be, in the world we inhabit now. Before diving into conversations about data-driven society, I wanted to turn the focus back to the act of datafication itself: the gap between a sign and what lies behind it, the texture of the process. It traces what resists being datafied: memory, experience, and the precise moments where digitization introduces loss.

Seven friends from different backgrounds — a music podcaster, artists, an improv performer, a business consultant, and others — joined the experiment. Through discussion, games, and hands-on exercises, each of us defined and demonstrated our own version of "datafication."

It began with a few questions of mine:
What is the difference between machine vision and human sight?
If I were recorded not by sensors but by the people closest to me — family, friends — captured through their eyes, preserved in their memory? What would that self record look like?
What does it actually mean to datafy oneself?
And what other imaginings of "datafication" are still possible?

工作坊的全过程都记录在 Milanote 上。"Working in Public"——让过程彼此可见——实际上同时连着三件我在乎的事：我怎么理解创造力、讨论的语境是怎么被塑造出来的，以及网络"异步"这一特性的潜力。

每周我们会围绕一个特定主题展开讨论，并完成一份"作业"——作业是我围绕"数据化与自我的关系"设计的一些游戏。参与者在平日里收集自己的素材，放在各自的工作间，成为讨论和共同创作的材料。

而在我的构想里，讨论其实从工作间就已经开始了。工作间让困惑变得可见，让思维在彼此之间流动；大家把思考摊在这里，相互评论、相互启发。我们能够一点点成为彼此的环境。

Week 1 - 概览Overview

第一周，我作为开场，给出的是整个工作坊的大背景思考——资料与方法论。它主要回应三个问题：
  • 我们如何理解数据和数据结构？
  • 它为什么重要？
  • 以及，我个人工作方法论的一些参考。
Mitchell 与 Thurtle 主编的 Data Made Flesh: Embodying Information 提供了这个工作坊最关心的核心命题：信息之所以能被普遍化、能脱离语境自由流通，本身就有一个需要被追问的前提——它预设了信息可以和身体、和它所诞生的具体情境相分离。
而数据与数据结构拥有其自身的美学和政治性，Matthew Fuller 的讲座解释了这背后的形式条件——数据如何被编码、被空间化地组织。他引入 Georges Perec 的 infra-ordinaire（次寻常）概念，这成为我把"日常实践"和"何为数据"接起来的关键节点。Oulipo 式扎根于日常、在规则约束下展开的创作，成为我方法论的核心基础。
而方法论的另一半，来自我对 protocol(协议/规程)的个人研究中，逐渐形成的一个信念：
理解是控制的前提条件之一。但看见不是。
允许自己看见他人，看见自己。
看见并不解决矛盾，而是吞并矛盾，让你成为他者。
比起"理解"（并进而控制），我更想要"看见"。这场工作坊真正的出发点，就是这种不急于消解矛盾、反而让自己被矛盾改变的关系。
In the first week, as an opening, I laid out the broader background of the whole workshop — its readings and its methodology. It mainly responds to three questions:
  • How do we understand data and data structures?
  • Why does it matter?
  • And some references for my own working methodology.
Data Made Flesh: Embodying Information, edited by Mitchell and Thurtle, offers the core proposition this workshop cares about most: the fact that information can be universalized and circulate freely, detached from context, itself rests on a premise that needs to be questioned — it assumes that information can be separated from the body, and from the concrete situation in which it was born.
Data and data structures, in turn, carry an aesthetics and a politics of their own. Matthew Fuller's lecture explains the formal conditions behind this — how data is encoded and spatially organized. He brings in Georges Perec's concept of the infra-ordinary, which became the key point where I connect "everyday practice" with "what counts as data." An Oulipo-like making — rooted in the everyday, unfolding under self-imposed constraints — forms the core of my methodology.
The other half of that methodology comes from a conviction that took shape during my own research into protocol:
Understanding is one of the preconditions of control. Seeing is not.
To allow yourself to see others, and to see yourself.
Seeing does not resolve contradiction; It absorbs contradiction into you, makes you become the other.
Rather than "understanding" (and, in turn, controlling), what I want is to "see." The true starting point of this workshop is exactly this kind of relation — one that does not rush to dissolve contradiction, but lets itself be changed by it.

Week 3 - 环境Environment

第三周记录：本周主题"环境"与"无意识"
用以下任意一种方式，捕捉你周围的环境:
  • 照片——任何吸引你的东西，在任何安全的公共空间（街道、公园、商场……）。大约拍 10 张。
  • 声音——录下抓住你注意力的声响，剪辑到 5 分钟以内。
  • 你自己的方式——发明一种属于你自己的捕捉方法。
"环境"和"无意识"这两个关键词怎么理解都可以——就从这两个词出发，自由探索和创作。
（右滑查看所有图片→)
Weekly brief: this week's theme — "Environment" & "Unconsciousness"
Capture your surroundings in one of these ways:
  • Photos — anything that catches your eye, in any safe, public place (street, park, mall…). Aim for about 10.
  • Sound — record what catches your ear; clip it to under 5 minutes.
  • Your own way — invent your own method of capturing.
"Environment" and "unconsciousness" are open to interpretation — explore and create from these two words however you like.

Week 4 - 例外The Exception

第四周记录：借由彼此来"采集"数据
  • 交给别人记录你。不再自己记录，而是通过一次真人互动，让朋友来采集你的数据。数据尽量简单——一个词的心情、体温、位置，任何可以被记下来的小东西。
  • 选择你的互动方式。对方在你身边，还是在远方？在身边，比如请朋友握一下你的手，记下手相握时的温度；在远方，比如用微信（异步留言），或电话（即时、会打断、私人空间半敞开的状态）。
  • 互动本身就是重点。具体怎么记，由你们自己设计；数据，由对方来记录和保存。

我把这一周称为"例外"，因为收集数据本身，在这里变成了一场和朋友们一起的游戏。

数据平时总是被无意识地记录着；而朋友的一句要求，把这个过程翻转成了一次有意识的交出。交出的同时，数据多了一层语境——它因为我和这位朋友的关系才得以出现；我自己的状态也被改变了：我必须始终向外敞开，向着被打扰的可能敞开。

我选择的数据是心跳。心跳自身就带着私密性，它是身体的，也是物质的。我们用各种方式去转换它——声音、影像、绘画——这些方法自带游戏性，但它们回应的是同一个问题：回到心跳成为数字之前。我们凝视的，是心跳在身体里震动的时刻，是它与人的状态、与我和他人的关系相连的时刻。

比如：朋友叫我之后，我手搭脉搏，把心跳的节奏唱给她听，由她记下；或者把脉搏贴在水杯上，录下水面的震动发过去。Fori 数下"16 下 / 18 秒"——对同一个心跳，她写下"静止"，我写下"微弱"。记录需要精确，过程却极其随性：一种"随意的精确"。

越敏的记录方式：音频。竟然是转换方式——用可以录入、转交给我听到的方式记录下心跳。

Weekly brief: "collecting" data through one another
  • Let someone else record you. Instead of recording yourself, have a friend collect your data through a real interaction. Keep the data simple — a one-word mood, body temperature, a location, any small thing that can be noted down.
  • Choose how you interact. Is the other person near you or far away? Up close — ask a friend to hold your hand and note the warmth of that grip. At a distance — through WeChat (an asynchronous message), or a phone call (immediate, interrupting, private space left half-open).
  • The interaction is the point. How you record is yours to design; the data is recorded and kept by the other person.

I call this week "the exception," because here, collecting data itself became a game — one played together with friends.

Data is usually recorded without our awareness; a friend's request flips that process into a conscious handing-over. And in handing it over, the data gains a layer of context — it exists only because of my relationship with this particular friend. My own state changes too: I have to stay open outward, open to the possibility of being interrupted.

The data I chose was my heartbeat. A heartbeat carries its own intimacy; it is bodily, and material. We converted it in all kinds of ways — sound, video, drawing — methods playful in themselves, yet all answering the same question: returning to the heartbeat before it becomes a number. What we were gazing at was the moment a heartbeat trembles inside a body — the moment it connects to a person's state, and to my relations with others.

For instance: when my friend called on me, I rested my fingers on my pulse and sang its rhythm to her, for her to write down; or I pressed my pulse against a glass of water and filmed the trembling of its surface. Fori counted "16 beats / 18 seconds" — and for the same heartbeat, she wrote "still" while I wrote "faint." The recording had to be precise; the process was entirely casual: a kind of "casual precision."

Yuemin's way of recording: audio. A conversion, in fact — recording the heartbeat by making it something that can be sung, handed over, and heard.

…
With different ways of converting, even our adjectives for the heartbeat began to change.
— Fori: still; Yuemin: faint.
